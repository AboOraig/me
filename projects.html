    Uncertainty-Aware Multimodal Inference under Sensor Degradation

    A controlled synthetic study of multimodal state estimation from three heterogeneous sensors under noise, bias faults, dropout, heavy-tailed noise, correlated multi-sensor failure, quality-signal corruption and deceptive sensors.

    Details
    • Developed a five-method baseline ladder (fixed WLS, sensor-quality heuristic, learned fusion, learned per-sensor uncertainty, and oracle variance) under a 5-seed protocol with fixed evaluation sets. The learned uncertainty model nearly matched the heuristic (RMSE: 0.705 vs 0.701), while variance shuffling confirmed informative predictions (Pearson r = 0.73, Spearman ρ = 0.74).
    • Evaluated cross-sensor residual features and Huber/Tukey robust reweighting as alternatives to the heuristic; both yielded negative results.
    • Under severe quality-signal corruption, the learned model outperformed the heuristic (RMSE: 1.153 vs 1.328, ~13% lower), while fixed weighting performed best (0.838). The proposed explanation, bounded predicted variance, remains untested.
    • Used NEES-based calibration analysis to show a fivefold increase in calibration error under variance-matched heavy-tailed noise (ECE: 0.025 to 0.129), despite unchanged predicted sharpness. Scalar moment-matched recalibration failed to correct the error, consistent with a tail-shape mismatch.
    • Implemented the model in NumPy with hand-derived backpropagation, validated using finite differences and cross-checked the core model in PyTorch. Published the code and a tagged release.

    • PyTorch

    Learned Regularization for Sparse-View CT: Plug-and-Play Prior vs. Tuned Total Variation

    A controlled study of when a learned image prior helps sparse-view CT reconstruction, and when it does not.

    Details
    • Designed a three-stage Plug-and-Play proximal-gradient study using a noise-conditioned DnCNN prior, progressing from 28x28 synthetic digits to 362x362 LoDoPaB-CT slices with simulated low-dose measurements. Implemented a sparse-view Radon operator with exact adjoint, a matrix-free parallel-beam operator validated against its dense counterpart, and a Poisson Beer--Lambert model. Benchmarked against validation-tuned FBP, Tikhonov, and unconstrained and box-constrained TV.
    • Evaluated 30, 60, and 120 projection angles across three dose levels using patient-disjoint CT splits (20 validation slices per condition; 40 test patients). Beyond PSNR/SSIM, assessed synthetic-lesion detection (paired AUC with a location-known observer) and false-positive lesion-like structures, while controlling for the inverse crime, denoiser seeds, and matched-prior effects.
    • On synthetic digits, the learned prior improved PSNR by 3.4dB over box-constrained TV at moderate noise, but reversed under distribution shift (−3.1dB for an MNIST-trained prior on Fashion-MNIST; +2.2dB with a Fashion-trained prior). On CT, PnP differed from 300-iteration TV by −0.93 to +0.33dB across nine dose-angle conditions, with lower SSIM in 8 of 9, highlighting limited transfer of the learned prior.
    • Achieved higher lesion-detection AUC than 300-iteration TV in all nine conditions (significantly in six), but never significantly exceeded FBP. PSNR-tuned TV also underperformed FBP in detection across all conditions. The PnP advantage was significant in only one of six non-anatomical phantom conditions, highlighting the difference between image quality and detectability. Limitations include one small denoiser, 40 CT patients, synthetic lesions, and no reader study.

  • Learning-Augmented Branch-and-Bound for Mixed-Integer Optimization

    A machine-learning branching rule for SCIP that imitates strong branching, evaluated against classical rules on unseen set-covering instances, with a technical report.

    Details
    • Trained a LightGBM ranking model to imitate strong branching from 19 cheap features and plugged it into SCIP (PySCIPOpt) as a custom branching rule, benchmarked against most-fractional, pseudocost and reliability branching on set-covering instances.
    • On 80 unseen instances (cuts and presolve off): 39-48% less solve time than reliability branching, about 4x faster than most-fractional (test set), close to pseudocost (20% fewer nodes); at a 10s budget solved 16/30 large instances vs. 9/30 for reliability branching.

  • Mixed-Integer Optimization for EV Charging

    Scheduling electric-vehicle charging under charger, time-window, and demand constraints as a resource-constrained optimization problem.

    Details
    • Formulated electric-vehicle charging allocation as a mixed-integer optimization problem (resource-constrained scheduling) with charger, time-window, and user-demand constraints; solved to global optimality using the open-source HiGHS solver, scaling to 100+ vehicles in under 0.1s.
    • Analyzed trade-offs between energy cost, charging demand, and user waiting time, showing how resource availability (charger count) reshapes the achievable cost–waiting Pareto frontier.
    • Extended the deterministic formulation to a two-stage stochastic optimization framework capturing uncertainty in vehicle arrival times and energy requirements; validated via out-of-sample testing that the stochastic policy outperforms a deterministic point-forecast policy (positive Value of the Stochastic Solution).
    • Implemented the full framework in Python and evaluated nominal and uncertainty-aware solutions through computational experiments (scalability, cost–waiting trade-offs, out-of-sample policy comparison), producing reproducible results and visualizations.
    • Python
    • Optimization
    • Mixed-Integer Programming
